In deserts, ruined portals are often buried in sand, and you only see their top half. Read the visible
shape to know where the chest is, and dig straight to it.

## How it works

- Portals are randomly mirrored and rotated. Look for the asymmetric parts you can see: slab overhangs,
  a taller pillar, iron bars, stone-brick walls.
- Slabs in the original structure stay slabs. In deserts, stairs can turn into slabs, but slabs never
  turn into stairs.
- Rules of thumb:

| Type | Chest |
| --- | --- |
| Small U | tall-pillar side |
| Hanging | opposite the slab overhang and the pillar |
| C-Dot | side of the small structure |
| Big U | smaller pillar's side |

- When only one axis is asymmetric, there are two candidate spots: check both.
- A sunken patch of sand often marks a lava source or a gold block that generated as air.
- For big portals, skip the chest unless you have memorised it.

## How to practice

1. Learn the rules for the small types first; they are 95% of ruined portals.
2. On buried portals, mark your guess, dig, and check.
