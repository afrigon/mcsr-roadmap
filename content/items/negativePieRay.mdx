Sometimes the pie-ray gives a "diagonal spike": you cross two borders with nothing, raise render distance by one, and the spawner loads, leaving you unsure where it is. The negative pie-ray (Mar1n) settles it by unloading the spawner instead of loading it.

## How it works

- After a diagonal spike, lower render distance by 3. The spawner chunk is now barely loaded, at
  the edge of your render distance.
- Cross a chunk border. If the spawner unloads, you just walked away from it, so it lies in the
  opposite direction, with certainty.
- Mar1n's three outcomes:

| Result | Meaning |
| ------ | ------- |
| The spawner never unloads | It really is diagonal (case 5). |
| It unloads after you go back one chunk | Case 6.1 in the video. |
| It unloads after you go back to the original chunk | Case 6.2 in the video. |

- Mar1n rates the standard pie-ray as enough on about 95% of seeds; the negative version is for
  the confusing rest.

## How to practice

1. In the pie-ray simulator map, run the standard scan until you get a diagonal spike.
2. Switch to the negative steps there and confirm the answer by walking to the spawner.
3. Watch Mar1n's video once per case and match each case to what you saw.
