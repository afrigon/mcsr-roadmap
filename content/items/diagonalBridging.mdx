Diagonal bridging takes you corner to corner in a straight diagonal line. It is faster than bridging
along one axis, turning, and bridging along the other, which is useful when you are heading for
coordinates or a bastion over lava.

## How it works

- Line up just to one side of the block's corner (Calisthenicuber stands barely right of it).
- Place two blocks almost at once at the corner, then release crouch and move to the next corner.
- Don't alternate the strafe keys between blocks. Hold the same keys and let the double placement
  carry you along the diagonal.
- Place with a butterfly click (two fingers alternating on one button) or a drag click. A double click
  that misfires drops you off the bridge.
- Crouch timing is slightly slower than on a straight speed bridge, because each step is longer.
- **Inclined diagonal**: add a jump to step up one block each time. It replaces bridging straight,
  turning and then pillaring.

## How to practice

1. Learn the flat diagonal first, in a creative world, until you can do ten corners without falling.
2. Add the step up only after the flat version is reliable.
3. Practice heading toward a fixed coordinate so you learn to correct the line mid-bridge.
