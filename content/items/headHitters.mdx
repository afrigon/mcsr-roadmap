A head hitter is a low ceiling over your path. Sprint-jumping under one is faster than sprint-jumping
in the open, so runners look for them in tunnels, under leaves and along structure edges.

## How it works

- Each sprint jump gives a burst of forward speed when you take off.
- With a block above your head, the jump is cut short and you land sooner. You take off again sooner,
  so you get that burst more often.
- A 2-block-tall tunnel or a low ceiling of leaves gives this effect. The exact speed gain and the best
  ceiling height were not found in a speedrun source.

## How to practice

1. Dig a long 2-tall tunnel in a practice world and sprint-jump through it, then sprint-jump the same
   distance in the open and compare.
2. In real runs, steer under leaves and overhangs when they are on your line anyway.
