# Zero cycle

A zero cycle kills the dragon on its initial flight: pearl to the obsidian tower under the first node,
build a setup, and explode beds and anchors each time the dragon passes, before it ever perches.

This file is anchored on two sources the roadmap owner rates as very reliable: **pion's YouTube channel
(thepionsneer)**, together with pion's own coordinate sheet, and **ClearColdWater's lenience sheet**.
Older sources (doogile, kohout135, Couriway's guide, hasbiWalks) are kept where the anchor sources are
silent, and every disagreement is listed under "Corrections to existing pages".

Tags: **[C: source]** confirmed by that source; **[U]** unconfirmed (inferred, or a single unclear
source). Coordinates are block coordinates, X then Z (the integer `Block:` line in F3).

Tower names follow the roadmap. Sources write them as S76 (Small Boy), C79 (Small Cage), C82 (Tall
Cage), M85, M88, M91, T94, T97, T100 and T103 (Tall Boy).

## Notation and terms

- **N+M** [C: pion titles and descriptions]: N is the number of dragon rotations (passes) used, M the
  number of *extra* explosives chained into a pass (each "+1" lands right after another explosive).
  Total explosives = N + M. pion: "2+2 still equals a 4 explosive zero cycle"; "4 rotation 5 bed"
  is a 4+1; "A REAL 2+1 ZERO CYCLE" is three explosives; "2+3" is five explosives on two rotations
  (so one pass carries a triple); 5+2 is seven. This matches the Mescht practice map's `X+Y` count.
- **5+0 / "5 explo" / bowless**: five passes, one explosive each.
- **XbYa** [C: pion]: X beds and Y anchors, for example 4b1a, 3b2a, 1b4a.
- **Hybrid / modern** [C: pion sheet]: hybrid setups have the bed facing north–south, modern setups
  east–west. pion: a hybrid is "a 'hybrid' of the original twagz setup and the astra/modern setup by
  taking the standing coords and orientation of twagz and combining them with the build methods of
  astra". For multiblock setups the hybrid/modern label names which of the two bed positions is the
  main one if you build a plain two-obsidian setup instead.
- **Multiblock** [C: pion sheet]: three obsidian give two bed positions on both axes; the
  "multiblock adjusted" standing block is diagonal to both. You must memorise which bed to use per
  rotation. pion: multiblock "is not really necessary" on normal setups, because the plain hybrid
  stands reach 2+2 by shooting the dragon to adjust height.
- **Offset** [C: pion comment]: further from the crystal horizontally because the standing height is
  too low to be far enough vertically. **Side**: below the top of a tall tower. **Universal side**:
  one standing block that works on several towers at one height.
- **Bed extension / double extension** [C: pion sheet]: a second bed position; `x y z**` marks a
  double extension.
- **Fast / slow / centerline** [C: pion descriptions 2026-06-21, 2026-07-07, 2026-07-30]: each
  rotation direction has a fast and a slow case. pion gives CW fast 18%, CW slow 32%, CCW fast 18%,
  CCW slow 32%. **Centerline** is a subcase of slow CW.
- **Queer arrow / trans arrow** [C: pion description 2026-06-18]: a queer arrow hits the dragon at the
  start of its rotation; a trans arrow hits it towards the end of the previous rotation.
- **O level**: on a buried spawn, the y of the highest solid block above you (shown as "O:" on the
  practice map's overlay) [C: pion comment].

## Findings

### Tower identification

- Ten towers; top heights 76 (Small Boy), 79 (Small Cage), 82 (Tall Cage), 85, 88, 91, 94, 97, 100,
  103 (Tall Boy) [C: pion sheet tab names]. The cages carry iron bars: the lenience sheet uses 83 as
  Small Cage's height and 86 as Tall Cage's for pearl and build time [C: lenience sheet, "c79(83)",
  "c82(86)"].
- pion groups them as **Small** (Small Boy, Small Cage, Tall Cage), **Medium** (M-85, M-88, M-91) and
  **Tall** (T-94, T-97, T-100, Tall Boy) [C: pion sheet]. Tall Cage counts as a small tower.
- Small Boy is never the 1/8 tower [C: lenience sheet edit 2026-03-05: "Removed s76 in 1/8 because it
  apperantely doesn't exist"; it adds that every tower then has a different chance of being the 1/8
  tower].
- Telling the tall towers apart: pion's only advice is practice ("i can identify between the tall
  towers fairly accurately ... 'cause i've been practicing it on and off for 15 months") [C: pion
  comment]. Workarounds he gives [C: pion comments]:
  - On a side approach it rarely matters: universal side setups use the same coordinates on every
    tower they cover.
  - Going to y99 "makes it pretty obvious which tower is which".
  - At y95 you see the tower's height while building, and pick T-94 y95 or the y95 universal side.
  - For the tallest towers you can pearl to the top first and then pearl down onto the side setup;
    "it doesn't waste too much time".
- The non-offset rule ties the tower to a height: you can stand non-offset when your standing height
  is at least 5 above the tower's height; any lower and you must offset, or the bed breaks the crystal
  and the dragon strafes [C: pion comment]. pion's setup list fits this using the obsidian height
  (Small Cage y86 is non-offset, Tall Cage y86 is an offset).

### Heights

- **Diagonal standing heights**: y81, 86, 90, 95, 99, 104 (and y108 on front Tall Boy) [C: pion
  sheet]. "Other heights" carry a bed-break chance or are hard to reach: y82 about 10%, **y87 about
  25%**, y91 about 10%, y100 under 5% [C: pion sheet]. pion on y91: about 10% "by default (depends on
  rotation as the dragon approaches from a different direction)"; some players prefer y90 one block
  more offset, at slightly worse damage [C: pion comment].
- **y100 vs y99**: the y100 bed break depends on fast or slow dragons; "if you see a slow dragon you
  can play it safe and go for y99" [C: pion comment]. On back T-97 y100 the break "can [happen] but
  especially on back it's quite rare" [C: pion comment].
- **y86**: "the dragon will fly away a lot from y86, that's just a guarantee"; flyaway odds are the
  same front and back; pion's own fix is y90, which also gives more consistent damage [C: pion
  comment].
- **y81**: about a 5% combined chance over all terrain heights that the dragon stays [C: pion comment].
- **No-pearl curiosity**: back Small Boy y79 offset with a y73 node has a 5% chance not to fly away
  and needs terrain below y59 [C: pion description, 2025-07-17].
- **How high non-offset goes**: y104 works up to front Small Cage and back M-91 non-offset; with the
  side tower, y104 on front Small Boy [C: pion comment, untested by him: "no but i've been very close
  before"]. Back Tall Boy has no y108 setup ("NOPE") [C: pion sheet].
- **Lower is easier, but flies away more**: off offset setups, "a lower height is essentially
  guaranteed to be easier than a higher one", but low heights fly away much more often, and offset
  setups have harder timing because the dragon circles further from the beds [C: pion comment].
- **1/8 heights**: pion lists 78, 83, 87, 92, 96, 101 (105 as "other"), all **triple-staircase**
  heights; the heights in brackets in his video are the double-staircase equivalents [C: pion sheet and
  comment]. The lenience sheet uses 84, 88, 92/93, 96/97, 102 [C: lenience sheet], matching the
  double-staircase set; 92 and 93 differ by at most 0.05 s [C: lenience sheet note]. Pearling to about
  y93 is "the sweet spot" for the 1/8 tower pearl [C: lenience sheet note].

### Standing blocks and offsets: the pattern

- Moving one block changes everything: T-100 y104 has to be one block offset, because at 28 104 −26
  "the bed breaks the crystal and the dragon flies" [C: pion comment].
- The lenience sheet notes offsets as moves from its reference stands: `^` one block in −x (front) or
  +x (back), `>` one block in −z (front) or +z (back), `*` one diagonal (`^` and `>`). Its reference
  stands are hybrid 28 −26 / −30 25, multiblock 29 −29 / −30 27, universal side 28 −30 / −29 29
  [C: lenience sheet notes].
- Modern offsets cluster on 29 −29 / −30 28 (M-88 y90, T-97 y99, Tall Boy y94) and on the universal
  28 −30 / −29 29 (M-91 y91, T-100 y100) [C: pion sheet; the grouping is an observation].
- Builds are free: "you can do the builds however you want as long as the standing block and bed
  positions are the same" [C: pion comment].
- Older setups (mouseypc's tutorial, about 2023): "fine for the most part (some top runners still do
  them), i personally just don't like them because the bed damage on some rotations can be
  inconsistent" [C: pion comment].

### Building: triple stacking

- pion's build pattern: two diagonal staircases up and a **triple stack** of obsidian. He uses it for
  multiblock setups (to reach a standing block diagonal to both beds) and for speed. "A normal
  staircase into a diagonal staircase" means the fast triple-stack build on a setup that doesn't need
  multiblock, finished as a normal two-obsidian setup. On y104 he usually does normal staircase →
  diagonal staircase → triple stack, "helpful for some extra speed since y104 can be tough"; on hybrid
  y104, "one out and up one diagonal and triple stack" [C: pion comments].
- On 1/8, pion's heights assume three staircases then the obsidian setup [C: pion comment].
- The triple stack is the make-or-break step on fast setups: of the no-pearl back y81 3+2, "i just hit
  it everytime if i get the triple stack off" [C: pion, 2026-03-18].
- Time model [C: lenience thread]: each block stacked up 0.45 s, each block bridged 0.40 s, each
  diagonal bridge 0.50 s, staircasing toward the coordinates fully overlapped by stacking. A normal
  setup takes 2.05 s from start to bed placed, a multiblock setup 2.50 s.
- Misplace backups: none of the anchor sources describe them [U]. pion's 2025-04-19 clip shows "that
  bed extension misplace" and still reaches 4+1, with no explanation.

### Bed and anchor timing

- **Damage target**: aim for about 45–50 damage per bed. "if the dragon's head is next to the mattress
  of the bed it should be fine to blow it up as long as it isn't too low" [C: pion comment]. "the head
  of the bed is what does damage" [C: pion, explainer transcript].
- **Knockback rule**: "bedding early = less knockback and bedding late = more knockback". If the dragon
  is low, bed a little later; if it is higher, bed quite early. "it's a balancing act" [C: pion
  comment]. Knockback keeps the dragon high enough for the next pass.
- **Latest bed placement** per height [C: lenience sheet "bed place" column, seconds; the time origin
  is not stated and is probably End entry, see Unconfirmed]: diagonal y86 27.60, y90 24.65 (24.35
  M-88 multiblock, 24.25 universal), y95 20.80 (20.40–20.70 for offsets), y99 17.40 (17.00–17.20
  offsets and sides), y104 14.20 (13.90–14.10 offsets). Straight y84 28.95, y88 25.70, y92/93 22.50,
  y96/97 19.05, y102 15.60. ClearColdWater "eyeballed" these on the **faster** rotation of each
  height's hybrid setup, as the latest bed that still gives acceptable damage and knockback, and
  assumed each diagonal of offset makes the dragon arrive 0.1–0.2 s sooner (about ±0.3 s error)
  [C: lenience thread].
- **After an arrow**: delay the double bed slightly after the arrow hits; a double bed right after the
  hit does less knockback, because the dragon is more affected by the arrow slowdown [C: pion
  explainer].
- **Anchors**: pion plays 4b1a, 3b2a, 2b3a, 1b4a and 0b5a and plans a video on anchor offsets for the
  back y95 universal side [C: pion comment and explainer]. "non-preplaced anchors kinda suck for
  damage" [C: pion run description, 2025-07-05]. For the y95 back universal, the −28 29 stand is
  "anchor-friendly", built for "consistent 4b2a 3b3a and 2b4a" (frontcage thread title linked in the
  description) [C: pion].

### Bow, +1s and rotation control

- **Arrow and rotation flip** [C: pion comment]: "shoot dragon early = no flip, shoot dragon later =
  flip, shooting multiple times also usually flips it unless all of the shots are super early." For a
  deliberate flip, wait until the dragon is nearly over the bed.
- **Turning arrow** [C: pion, 2026-05-25]: shooting the dragon while it is turning shortens its path,
  so it reaches the bed sooner. The dragon descends according to its path length, so the shorter path
  cancels the extra descent from arrow slowdown. This makes double bow on y95 and 2+2 consistent.
  With the first arrow during the turn, the dragon does a tighter turn and is better placed for a
  second-rotation double; later arrows are for height [C: pion, 2026-04-11].
- **Trans arrow** [C: pion, 2026-06-18]: can change the dragon's rotations (y99 CW became y90 CCW in his
  clip); "extremely risky", "inconsistent, but can be very very powerful".
- **Double arrow**: usually improvised ("the dragon is a bit high up, lemme just shoot it twice"), but
  predetermined on some setups, for example back T-97 y99 hybrid CCW (two shots on rotation 2 to flip
  it on rotation 3) and back Tall Boy y104 non-multiblock CCW (two early shots on rotation 2 to angle
  the head toward the bed without a flip) [C: pion comment]. On hybrid setups 2+2 works on every case;
  double arrow is needed on y90 and y99 mainly on rotation 1, and the queer arrow helps on y95 CCW
  [C: pion comment].
- **Learning +1s**: "not double bedding wildly, practice double bedding when the dragon is at a good
  height and slowly work on being able to double bed on any rotation" [C: pion comment].

**Universal side 3+2 bed orders** [C: pion descriptions]. Left and right are the two bed positions as
seen by the player; "Double" means a +1 on that rotation.

**Reading the case on back y95 universal** [C: pion explainer, 2026-09-19]:
- Read the case as the dragon flies over the setup, not from its spawn angle: on buried spawns you
  cannot see the spawn, so practising the angle read doesn't transfer.
- CW: fast turns outward, far offset from the right bed on rotation 1, and comes back to the right bed
  on rotation 2; slow passes close to the right bed and turns inward to the left bed. Fast has worse
  rotation-1 damage and better rotation-3 damage than slow.
- On rotation 3 (both CW cases, mainly slow), **delay the arrow** as long as possible: the arrow makes
  the dragon veer left, and shot late it veers while still over the bed head. Shooting too early is
  "the main thing I see people choking slow clockwise 3+2".
- CCW: fast approaches the first bed diagonally, slow approaches parallel to it; parallel is more
  common. Judge by approach angle, not by distance. Rotations 1–2 on fast CCW are only for alignment
  and knockback; rotation 3 does most of the damage, usually with a double bow.
- If you got too low after rotation 2, shoot once and delay the double bed so the dragon turns into the
  beds. Too much knockback: triple bow on rotation 3.
- CCW 3+2 without a first-rotation double is much harder; time the second arrow to hit as the dragon
  reaches the beds on rotation 2 to get "the good flip" on rotation 3; harder on fast than on slow. It
  doesn't need multiblock. "the good flip is 100% consistent if you time your double/triple shot
  correctly" (2026-04-30); the second bed of the rotation-2 double can be blocked by the middle
  obsidian.

**Other 3+2 failure notes** [C: pion, 2025-06-02, back Tall Boy y104]: CW is "super easy ... if your
first bed isn't late"; if the dragon turns right instead of left on rotation 3 of CW, the bow shot was
too early, rotation 3 does little damage, and you settle for a 4+2 by doubling on rotation 4. CCW is
more random and often ends 4+2.

### Recovering a failing zero

The anchor sources say little here; most recovery content is still from Couriway's guide (Metafy).
- **Lower bed to recover height** [C: Metafy, from earlier research]: dragon too low → bed 1–2 blocks
  lower beside the obsidian, or drop down and bed where you stood. pion's closest advice is the
  knockback rule (bed later when the dragon is low) and the 4+2 fallback above.
- **Correcting a late or early bed** [C: Metafy; consistent with pion's knockback rule]: bed early or
  late on the next pass; wait a rotation after an extremely late bed.
- **Bed-break cases**: "Queer Arrow Cases" (2026-08-09) has chapters for a bed break on CCW and CW y95
  hybrid (3:35, 7:31), on front y95 universal (12:41), and single and double bed breaks on back T-97
  y100 (18:05, 17:19). What pion does in them is only on screen [U]:
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4QbgcdfoTyU&t=215s
- **Air placing** and **placing on fire**: no anchor source or earlier source describes them; no
  search result names them [U].
- Crystal as a last explosive, chase-down, full bow, mining back anchors, clutch and pearl back up
  [C: Metafy, from earlier research].
- After a bad pearl on a tall tower, pearl to the top and then down onto the side setup [C: pion
  comment].

### When to go for a zero vs a one cycle

- pion's framing: push the highest height you can reach to minimise flyaways; the low offset variants
  exist "so that you can still zero even on the buried ends" [C: pion comment].
- Don't learn the catalogue: "just find ONE setup to focus on learning and disregard the rest of the
  video until you have another specific setup you want to learn" [C: pion comment]. "zero is not
  everyone's strat" [C: pion comment].
- Lowest-height-only zeros work "theoretically", but with many flyaways; pion doesn't recommend it
  [C: pion comment].
- Use the lenience sheet as the go list: a setup is a go when its value for your spawn is positive by
  more than your own extra time. Negative values are not impossible: soul sand alternation, a "god
  pearl" and late beds are not counted, and ClearColdWater hit open front M-85 y103, which would be
  −0.35 s [C: lenience sheet and thread]. r0hkx reports being "a little slower than the sheet
  predicts" [C: thread reply].
- A slow dragon on y100 is a reason to drop to y99 [C: pion comment].
- Earlier go/no-go rules (bail when the pearl breaks the crystal, low health on back, too late for the
  lowest safe height, 3 pearls on back) come from Couriway's guide [C: Metafy, earlier research].

### Earlier research (kept)

The sections below come from doogile, Couriway's guide (Metafy), kohout135 and hasbiWalks. Where they disagree with the anchor sources, "Corrections to existing pages" applies.

#### Mechanics

- **Front or back** is decided by the taller of the two middle pillars and is fixed per seed. Then the
  node is **diagonal** (7/8) or **straight** ("1/8", between the fountain and a middle pillar); that
  roll is not fixed per seed.
- Node positions (practice-map particles): front diagonal 28 −29, back diagonal −29 28, front straight
  20 0, back straight −20 0.
- The dragon targets a point a random number of blocks above the node's base and descends at constant
  speed. Reaching the target means a flyaway; destroying the tower's crystal means a strafe.
- Ranked limits the zero-cycle target offset to 0–15 blocks (vanilla 0–20) and fixes its sequence per
  seed, so the dragon descends further before leaving. Low heights are relatively more viable in Ranked
  than in RSG; no source changes any coordinates for Ranked.
- A bed explodes at its pillow, which should sit on the node. An anchor explodes from its own block, so
  time anchors slightly earlier.
- Rotation (clockwise or counter-clockwise) is 50/50, read from the approach direction. It does not
  change bed placement on normal setups; it does on offset, side and multiblock setups.
- Shooting the dragon early in a rotation keeps the rotation; shooting late (over the bed) flips it.

#### hasbiWalks multiblock set

Every setup is multiblock and claims better alignment with the actual node. The later doogile and Metafy
guides still use the standard coordinates, so it is an optional optimization. It requires memorizing the
bed side per rotation for every tower.

#### Building the setup

- Standard: staircase up twice, bridge out one block, place two blocks with an indestructible block on
  top, step back, place a second indestructible block; the bed goes between them.
- Indestructible blocks: obsidian, crying obsidian or respawn anchors.
- Optimal: triple-stack build, with backups for misplaced blocks.
- Pearl onto the tower with a standstill jump pearl that does not hit the crystal; hitting it almost
  always causes a flyaway.

#### Damage variants

- **5+0**: five singles, the baseline. Every non-offset setup can be five-bedded without a bow. Aim
  for 5 even with spare explosives.
- **Anchors**: explode after the beds, since a mistimed anchor absorbs damage. They can replace the
  setup's obsidian; cover the facing side with a hard block (cobble, nether brick, not soul sand or
  netherrack). On tall towers the bottom anchor breaks the crystal, so it goes last. On T-94 back and all
  offset and side setups, anchors work only as the obsidian. For a bed + anchor double, place the anchor
  first.
- **Bow +1**: the arrow starts the dragon's 10-tick invulnerability timer and later hits don't reset
  it. Explode bed 1 just before the timer expires and bed 2 just after, for two near-full hits in one
  rotation. An arrow also removes the dragon's horizontal speed while it keeps descending, so shooting a
  low dragon breaks the bed. Bow only when the bed would have done good damage anyway.
- **Crossbow**: can be pre-loaded, but charges slower than a bow; the bow makes doubles easier.
- **3+2**: the realistic target for most players. Nearly as fast as 2+2, much easier.
- **2+2**: the top level, made consistent with **double shot** (extra arrows correct the dragon onto
  the node and shed excess height). For y95 counter-clockwise, use 27 −28 F / −29 26 B.
- **More than two explosives per rotation** (for example 5+1 universal back): exotic.

#### Spawn conditions

- **Open**: the reference case.
- **Overhang**: run to the back and pearl, or build up first; staircase out about 3 blocks for severe
  overhangs. Treat an overhang that encloses the platform as buried.
- **Buried**: mine at the back of the platform aiming backwards, turn around once the sky shows.

| O | Clip |
| - | ---- |
| ≤53 | No clip: mine and stack out, then pearl. |
| 54 | Mine 2. |
| 55 | Mine 3. |
| 56 | Mine 3, standstill pearl, hold jump (pitch about −82.5). |
| 57 | Mine 4, sneak, pearl, jump (shift clip); or build up 1 (normal clip). |
| 58+ | Shift clip: mine 5 and build 1 under, +1 per O level. Normal clip: build 2. |

Each O level costs about 1.1 s, which pushes you toward lower heights and offset or universal setups.
After a clip you can see whether it is a back dragon. A gold pickaxe reduces the time lost.

#### Inventory and preparation

- **Pre-crafted explosives**: craft before the End, overlapped with blazes.
- **Pre-sorted hotbar** (easy) vs **sorting between pearls** (optimal, "prepless"): only the pearls are
  guaranteed in the hotbar and everything else is sorted mid-pearl.
- Don't rebuild the hotbar for the zero; swap only what's needed so muscle memory holds.
- Tips: glowstone or crying obsidian in the obsidian slot, the bed in the offhand for doubles, a block
  under the top anchor to avoid being knocked off.
- **Health**: enter with full health and food, or hunger-reset with saturation. A death reset in the
  portal room is common and often the safest. Food in the offhand before the last eye skips the eating
  animation.
- **Eating mid-zero** keeps a low-health back-dragon zero alive (Metafy: survivable "if you eat food,
  use bow, or have good armor").
- Raise entity and render distance on entry; pause and unpause a desynced dragon.
- After the kill: boat down, or pillar 2 beside the fountain. Pearl or boat out of cages.

#### Go / no-go

- Read the node and rotation during the first pearl.
- Go when the tower × height has positive lenience for your speed and spawn, and you have enough
  explosives: 5 singles, about 4 with a bow, more for side setups without a bow.
- Bail when the pearl breaks the crystal, it's too late for the lowest safe height, health is low on
  back, or you have only 3 pearls on back (take only the easy setups).
- In real runs, take a one cycle on setups you can't hit yet.

#### Saving a failing zero (Metafy)

- Dragon too low: bed 1–2 blocks lower beside the obsidian, or drop down and bed where you stood.
- Bed early or late to correct the previous bed; wait a rotation after an extremely late bed.
- On tall towers, use the crystal as a last explosive; shoot nearby crystals for 10 damage.
- Chase-down: shoot the dragon and the crystals healing it.
- Full bow and wait for a perch; mine back anchors with a diamond pickaxe.
- Clutch and pearl back up; loop to a side setup after a bad pearl.

#### Fallbacks

One cycle, half bow, ground zero, or the damageless oneshot. See [end.md](end.md).

## Corrections to existing pages

Each item names the page or roadmap entry, what it says, and what the anchor sources say.

1. **N+M notation** (this file's earlier notation; followup-end.md item 17; threePlusTwo, doubleShot,
   twoPlusTwoY104 descriptions). Earlier: "M rotations with two explosives". pion: M counts extra
   explosives, so 2+3 is five explosives on two rotations and 2+1 is three. The two readings agree
   only while every +1 is a double. Use "N rotations, M extra explosives chained into them"; this
   also settles the conflict with the practice map's notation.
2. **bedTiming** ("Explode five beds one at a time ... each just as the previous hit's invulnerability
   ends ... space the five singles out evenly"). In a 5+0 each single goes on its own pass of the
   dragon, seconds apart; invulnerability only matters for +1s. pion's timing rule is the dragon's
   head next to the bed's head at about 45–50 damage, earlier for a high dragon and later for a low
   one, trading damage for knockback.
3. **Front 28 −26 called superseded** (frontLowY90: "Older guides use 28 −26 ... 28 −27 and y86 are the
   current values"; this file's earlier catalogue). pion's sheet lists 28 −26 **or** 28 −27 for
   every front non-offset setup, and the lenience sheet's front hybrid reference stand is 28 −26.
   Better: 28 −27 / −30 25 are pion's optimal **clockwise** stands, 27 −28 / −29 26 his optimal
   **counter-clockwise** stands; 28 −26 still works. The y86 part stands.
4. **universalSide description** ("works on T-94, T-97 and T-100"). Per pion's sheet the towers depend
   on the height: y86 M-88 to T-100, y90 M-91 to T-100, y95 T-97 and T-100, y99 T-100 and Tall Boy,
   and pion says it works on any tower. "Works on T-94 to T-100 at y90" or "from M-88 at y86 to Tall
   Boy at y99" fits better.
5. **Back Tall Boy y104** (earlier catalogue: "Practically never attempted"). pion: "back t103 top is
   secretly a free zero but literally no one tries to hit it" (2026-03-13), 4+1 on all four rotation
   cases (2025-04), consistent-ish 3+2 on both rotations (2025-06-02). Rare in practice but not a
   joke setup. Its lenience is 2.45 s open.
6. **Lenience page** ("Each O level of a buried spawn costs about 1.1 s"). The sheet's steps are
   uneven: open→O54 2.95 s, O54→O55 0.75, O55→O56 0.15, O56→O57 0.75, O57→O58 1.70; only O59–O61
   are 1.10 s each, and those are estimates.
7. **Pearl clip mining counts** (this file's earlier O-level table: O54 mine 2, O55 mine 3, ≤O53 mine
   and stack out). pion: O52 and O53 dig nothing (two-block pearl clip), O54 dig one, O55 dig two,
   O56 three, O57 four, O58 stack up one and dig five in total, one more per level. Aim −82 to −83
   at O56 and hold jump; at O57 and deeper aim about −85, crouch, pearl, uncrouch, hold jump. The
   lenience thread calls O54/O55 single pearl clips while pion's videos are titled "O53 Double Clip"
   and "O54 Double Clip", so the sources disagree on what single and double mean here.
8. **anchors** ("On back T-94 and on all offset and side setups, anchors work only as the obsidian").
   pion's back y95 universal stand −28 29 is built for anchors (the linked thread names 4b2a, 3b3a and 2b4a), with an
   upper-right anchor at −27 100 31 that breaks the crystal only on T-100. Scope the rule to the
   standard stands.
9. **m91** (front heights 95, 99). pion also lists front M-91 **y104** non-offset, and y91 and y90
   offsets. Front y95 is an offset in pion's terms ("y95 Offset", 28 −27 or 27 −26), which agrees with
   the page's "already one block out".
10. **t97** (front offset y99 "27 −28; some guides use 26 −27"). pion lists 29 −29 (modern,
    multiblock), 26 −27 (hybrid offset) and 28 −28 (hybrid multiblock), not 27 −28. The lenience
    sheet times the 29 −29 multiblock. 27 −28 comes from doogile and kohout only.
11. **t97BackY99** (kohout −30 29, Couriway −27 26, sheet −29 27). pion names them: −30 28 modern
    (multiblock), −27 26 hybrid offset (= Couriway), −29 27 hybrid multiblock (= the sheet). kohout's
    −30 29 is one block from pion's modern stand.
12. **t100Y100** (back −26 26). pion: back hybrid offset **−27 26** (multiblock adjusted, beds −26 28
    and −25 27), or the modern −29 29. −26 26 is in no anchor source. Front 25 −27 matches.
13. **tallBoyFrontY104** (29 −31, 26 −28, 29 −30, 25 −26). pion's three: 29 −30 modern, 25 −26 hybrid
    offset, **27 −28 hybrid multiblock**. Add 27 −28; 29 −31 and 26 −28 come only from the older
    sources.
14. **eighthLow / eighthHigh** ("remember them as one less (87, 91, 96): bridge at that height, then
    staircase up one"; "−24 0 ... probably the same block"). pion's one-less set (78, 83, 87, 92, 96,
    101) is the **triple-staircase** build, and 84/88/92/93/97/102 the double-staircase one: the
    build method shifts the height, not a bridge-then-step trick. pion's sheet gives back −23 0 (bed
    −21 0), which settles the −23 0 vs −24 0 question in favour of −23 0.
15. **towerIdentification** ("tower height + 5" from "the numbers in the names"). Confirmed by pion,
    using the obsidian height (Small Cage 79 and Tall Cage 82, not the bar tops). Worth adding pion's
    Small / Medium / Tall grouping and his side-approach tips (universal coordinates don't depend on
    the tower; y99 makes the tower obvious).
16. **frontLowY90 / backLowY90 height lists**. pion adds y81 and y99 on front Small Boy, y81 on back
    Small Boy and y99 on back Small Cage. pion would steer a y86 player to y90 (fewer flyaways, more
    consistent damage). Not wrong, only narrower.
17. **frontLowY90** ("Front is more forgiving than back because the front node is further from its
    tower"). pion: flyaway odds are the same front and back. The difference that shows in the
    numbers is time: back costs about 2.35 s more for the extra pearl. Not a direct contradiction;
    the reason given is unconfirmed.
18. **m88 back offset** (earlier catalogue: y91 at −29 27, pion −28 26). pion's sheet: y91 hybrid offset
    **−28 26**; y90 hybrid offset −28 27; y90 modern −30 28. −29 27 is in no anchor source.
19. **savingFailingZero** ("Top runners rarely give up on a zero ... there is almost always a way").
    No anchor source supports "almost always"; pion's failure notes end in 4+2 or 5+2 fallbacks, and
    he says that from low heights the dragon "will fly away a lot". Soften the claim.
20. **bowOffsetSide / bowPlusOne / goNoGo explosive counts** ("about 4 with a bow, more for side setups
    without a bow"). pion: every non-offset setup and every front offset can be five-bedded without a
    bow; back offsets can't consistently. So a no-bow back offset is the case that needs more
    explosives or a bow, not side setups in general.
